I saw an angel at the cemetery one night.<br />
It was kneeling at a gravestone, as if in prayer,<br /> 
staring up at a stone image of itself and it looked<br />
so lonely, <br />
I could feel it— <br />
I think it was trying to talk to the statue— <br />
its lips were moving,<br />
but I couldn’t hear a word.<br />
‘Hey man,” I told it, <br />
(<i>can you call an angel ‘man’?</i>)<br />
‘That’s not your friend. It’s not real, it can’t talk back.’<br />
It looked at me, head-on—at least I think, <br />
I couldn’t tell—   <br />
it was like looking into the sun. <br />
‘Just like Our Father,’ it said,<br />
voice buzzing like bees or flies or locusts.<br />
And then my head felt hot, like all of summer had burst behind my skull, <br />
and there was this sickening<br />
<i>pop</i><br />
somewhere behind my eyes.<br />
And that’s how I lost my ability to see the color blue.<br />
