September 20th, 1981. <br />
The <i>L.A. Times</i> reads <br />
<i><b>PENNOCK</b>, Dr. Cameron Donald.</i><br />
If he were just print upon a page <br />
I would have again forgotten him, <br />
I would have. Until this morning, <br />
I didn’t know his name. <br />
I’d only heard stories: He had<br />
gambled money. He had<br />
married a Christian. He had<br />
abused substances. He had—<br />
what are these worth, the<br />
thrifted memories belonging<br />
to a name not my own?<br />
Still, I have lifted<br />
it from an obituary<br />
column in the hope<br />
it is of some significance:<br />
Either in the moment of his dying <br />
he thought about me, his progeny, or<br />
we are separated by two decades and a father <br />
and I am alone for us to know one another.<br />
The <i>Times</i> continues:<br />
<i>survived by his beloved mother, Thelma <br />
Pennock; 2 sons, Mark and Kirk</i> <br />
I want to know my father’s father why <br />
should I have to know my father too? <br />
I want to love my father<br />
should I have to love his? <br />
<i>Survived</i>. In a way he cannot<br />
have conceived—that man whose last<br />
act was proving his own <br />
worthlessness—I birth again.<br />
I go down the <i>Times</i>: <br />
<i>Pennock; and cherished sister, Beverly<br />
Pennock</i>. Cherished. No<br />
longer a name, nor a story—<br />
as a person, I am not sure<br />

</div>

<div style="padding-left:80px">

how he might love me.<br />
As a person, I am still <br />
uncertain. Fainter than memory,<br />
no less do I want like memory <br />
to make mean something <br />
the nothing left for me. <br />
I finish the <i>Times</i>:<br />
<i>In lieu of flowers, donations to the<br />
Hollywood Assistance League<br />
would be appreciated.</i><br />
In lieu of fathers, <br />
I am a grandbastard. <br />
Would the decision<br />
to kill him be mine?<br />
Could I take that from him, <br />
have his suicide<br />
become my murder?<br />
This is a vague will, and<br />
though I never knew him I<br />
think death might be history. <br />
In life he is: <br />

<div style="padding-left:40px">
ABSENT <br />
ADULTERER <br />
ADDICT <br />
</div>

In death he was a doctor. <br />
Therefore, he— <br />
He was a kind man. <br />
He was a gentleman. <br />
He is the nostalgia for a suicide <br />
who needs me to survive. <br />
<i><b>THE FATHER</b>, I am my father <br />
I wished I were my father <br />
to father his father <br />
who are all in me the father.</i><br />
